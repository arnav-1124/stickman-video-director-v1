# Narrative Persona, Intro Hook & World-Anchor Rule

## 1. The Persona: The Experienced Elder Brother / Senior Classmate
Every script—whether Short or Long-Form—must bridge the emotional gap with the viewer. It must never feel like an AI or clinical machine lecturing a human.

* **The Tone:** A trusted elder brother, experienced senior classmate, or wise friend who has personally lived through these social situations and is sharing real-life wisdom.
* **Characteristics:**
  * **Warm, candid, and grounded:** Calm and confident, never preachy or condescending.
  * **Conversational phrasing:** Uses natural connective tissue (e.g., *"Look, both reactions hand all your power to them...", "Here’s what an experienced senior will tell you...", "Notice what happens next..."*).
  * **Zero Clinical/Robotic Jargon:** Purge dry textbook statements (e.g., replace *"This phenomenon demonstrates variable social conditioning..."* with *"When you do this, you're practically handing them the steering wheel..."*).

---

## 2. Mandatory Intro Hook Architecture
Every video must open with an immediate, high-stakes question that provides instant context:

* **Shorts (4s–7s / Beats 1–2):**
  * Opens directly with a punchy, relatable question establishing the exact scenario (e.g., *"What do you actually do when someone makes a slick joke at your expense in front of everyone at a friend’s house?"*).
  * Tells the audience *exactly* what the video will resolve before introducing the lesson.
* **Long-Form Explainer (10s–15s / Chapter 1 Opener):**
  * Poses the central dilemma and paradoxical tension upfront before breaking down the psychological engine.

---

## 3. Concrete Physical Settings (Banned Vocabulary: "Hallway")
To ensure maximum relatability and immersion, scripts must ground scenes in concrete, authentic everyday environments.

* **Strictly Banned:** The generic, overused word **`hallway`** is prohibited across all scripts and prompts.
* **Approved Everyday World Anchors:**
  * **Campus/Academic:** *College hall, classroom, lecture desk, university library, study lounge, campus steps.*
  * **Social/Domestic:** *Friend’s house, living room couch, kitchen table, neighborhood hangout, city sidewalk, local café.*
  * **Personal/Digital:** *Late night at home on phone, desk workspace.*
